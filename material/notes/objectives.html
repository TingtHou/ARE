PA Programming & Analysis — 16 objectives

ObjNCARB wordingWhat it actually asks
1 · Environmental & Contextual Conditions — 14–21% (≈11–16 items)
1.1Evaluate site-specific environmental and socio-cultural opportunitiesGiven a site, name what it offers: solar access, prevailing breeze, views, existing vegetation, neighbourhood character, walkability, cultural assets. Opportunity, not constraint.
1.2Evaluate site-specific environmental constraintsThe mirror image: slope, soils, water table, floodplain, wetlands, contamination, easements, noise, adjacent shading. Which one kills which programme element.
1.3Determine optimal use of onsite resources by incorporating sustainability principlesDaylight, solar orientation, stormwater capture, existing structures worth reusing, topography worth working with rather than against.
2 · Codes & Regulations — 16–22% (≈12–17 items)
2.1Identify relevant code requirements for building and site typesWhich occupancy group, which construction type, what the allowable height and area envelope is, whether sprinklers are required. Identify, at feasibility resolution.
2.2Identify relevant zoning and land use requirementsFAR, height, setbacks, lot coverage, parking ratios, use permissions. Read an ordinance excerpt and work out what fits on the lot.
2.3Identify relevant local and site-specific requirementsOverlays, historic districts, design review, wetlands buffers, utility easements, deed restrictions, the entitlement path.
3 · Site Analysis & Programming — 21–27% (≈16–20 items)
3.1Evaluate relevant qualitative and quantitative attributes of a site as they relate to a programDoes this site hold this programme? Buildable area after setbacks and slope, parking, access, servicing.
3.2Synthesize site reports with other documentation and analysisYou are handed a geotech report, a survey and an ESA that partly disagree. Reconcile them and say what governs.
3.3Analyze graphical representations regarding site analysis and site programmingRead a contour plan, a site section, a FIRM panel, a solar diagram. Hotspot items live here.
4 · Building Analysis & Programming — 37–43% (≈28–32 items) — the heaviest section in your three divisions
4.1Evaluate relevant qualitative and quantitative attributes of a new or existing building as they relate to the programNet vs gross, efficiency factor, floor-to-floor, structural bay, condition of an existing building against the programme it must hold.
4.2Evaluate documentation, reports, assessments, and analyses to inform the building programBuilding condition assessment, hazardous-materials survey, existing-conditions inventory, user questionnaires, occupancy data.
4.3Identify and prioritize components of the building programWhat is required, what is desirable, what is aspirational — and which gets cut when the budget does.
4.4Assess spatial and functional relationships for the building programAdjacency matrices, bubble and block diagrams, blocking and stacking, separated vs mixed flow.
4.5Review and assess the feasibility of the project budget and schedule to meet the project scope Narrowed Apr 2026You are given a budget and a schedule. Say whether the scope fits inside them. You are no longer asked to build the budget from scratch.
4.6Identify alternatives for building and structural systems for given programmatic requirements, preliminary budget, and scheduleLong span vs short span, steel vs concrete vs mass timber, against programme span needs, money and time. Your folder covers this badly.
4.7Analyze graphical representations regarding building analysis and building programmingRead an adjacency matrix, a stacking diagram, a net/gross plan and derive a number or a judgement from it.

PPD Project Planning & Design — 17 objectives

ObjNCARB wordingWhat it actually asks
1 · Environmental Conditions & Context — 10–16% (≈10–16 items)
1.1Determine location of building and site improvements based on site analysisWhere on the lot the building goes, and why — solar, slope, access, drainage, setbacks, views.
1.2Determine sustainable principles to apply to designPassive strategy by climate, envelope response, daylighting, water, the rating-system frameworks.
1.3Determine impact of neighborhood context on the project designMassing and scale against neighbours, shadow, street wall, contextual materials, transitions.
2 · Codes & Regulations — 16–22% (≈16–22 items)
2.1Apply zoning and environmental regulations to site and building designNot identify — apply. Compute what the envelope permits and shape the building to it.
2.2Apply building codes to building designOccupant load, egress width, exit count and separation, travel distance, allowable height and area, construction type.
2.3Integrate multiple codes to a project designIBC + accessibility + energy + fire, simultaneously, where they conflict. The more stringent governs.
3 · Building Systems, Materials & Assemblies — 19–25% (≈19–25 items)
3.1Determine mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systemsPick the system type for the building type and climate, and size its space demands.
3.2Determine structural systemsSpan, bay, depth, material, lateral system. Not covered by anything in your folder.
3.3Determine special systems such as acoustics, communications, lighting, security, conveying, and fire suppressionSix named systems. Complete-list territory — expect check-all-that-apply.
3.4Determine materials and assemblies to meet programmatic, budgetary, and regulatory requirementsEnvelope and interior assemblies judged on three axes at once: does it work, can you afford it, is it legal.
4 · Project Integration of Program & Systems — 32–38% (≈32–38 items) — the heaviest section in PPD
4.1Determine building configurationFootprint, massing, floor count, core position, bay rhythm — resolved against everything else at once.
4.2Integrate building systems in the project designWhere the ducts, the structure and the ceiling all fit. Floor-to-floor stack-ups.
4.3Integrate program requirements into a project designThe programme from PA, now made into a plan that also satisfies code and systems.
4.4Integrate environmental and contextual conditions in the project designOrientation, shading, prevailing wind, context, closed back into the design rather than treated as analysis.
5 · Project Costs & Budgeting — 8–14% (≈8–14 items)
5.1Evaluate design alternatives based on the programTwo schemes, one programme. Which serves it better and at what cost.
5.2Evaluate cost estimates based on the project design Narrowed Apr 2026Judge an estimate you are given — is it complete, is the accuracy right for the phase, is anything missing. You no longer build it.
5.3Evaluate the project design based on cost considerations Clarified Apr 2026Which design move saves money, and what it costs in performance. Value engineering reasoning.

PDD Project Development & Documentation — 17 objectives

ObjNCARB wordingWhat it actually asks
1 · Integration of Building Materials & Systems — 31–37% (≈31–37 items)
1.1Analyze the integration of architectural systems and technologies to meet project goalsDoes this assembly, in this building, with these other systems, actually work.
1.2Determine the size of mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems and componentsDuct, shaft, chase, equipment room, panel, riser — dimensions, not concepts. Gap in your folder.
1.3Determine the size of structural systemsMember depth, bay, slab thickness, footing. Gap in your folder.
1.4Integrate specialty systems such as acoustics, lighting, fire suppression, conveying, security, and communicationsSame six as PPD 3.3 but at detail resolution — where the sprinkler head lands relative to the light and the diffuser.
1.5Determine how to detail the integration of multiple building systems and technologiesDraw the joint. Envelope transitions, control layers, flashing, movement.
1.6Coordinate mechanical, electrical, plumbing, structural, and specialty systems and technologiesClash resolution, penetrations through structure, firestopping, coordination drawings.
2 · Construction Documentation — 32–38% (≈32–38 items) — the heaviest section in PDD
2.1Determine appropriate documentation of building designWhat gets drawn where: plan, section, elevation, schedule, detail.
2.2Determine appropriate documentation of site featuresCivil, landscape, grading, utilities — what belongs on whose sheet.
2.3Determine appropriate documentation of detailed building drawings within individual architectural systemsWall sections, details, enlarged plans, and their reference conventions.
2.4Apply standards required to assemble a set of clear and coordinated construction documentationNational CAD Standard, UDS, sheet order, layering, abbreviations, keynotes, BIM/LOD.
2.5Determine impact of project changes on documentation requirements and methods to communicate those changesAddendum vs ASI vs change order vs CCD vs bulletin — which instrument, when, and what it does to the set.
3 · Project Manual & Specifications — 12–18% (≈12–18 items)
3.1Identify and prioritize components required to write, maintain, and refine project manualDivision 00 vs Division 01, bidding requirements, contract forms, conditions.
3.2Identify and prioritize components required to write, maintain, and refine project specificationsMasterFormat, SectionFormat's three parts, PageFormat, the four methods of specifying.
3.3Coordinate specifications with construction documentationWhat goes in the drawings vs the specs, and what happens when they conflict.
4 · Codes & Regulations — 8–14% (≈8–14 items)
4.1Determine adherence to building regulatory requirements (IBC) at detail levelRated assembly, opening protective, penetration, damper, fireblocking — at the detail, not the plan.
4.2Determine adherence with specialty regulatory requirements at the detail levelAccessibility dimensions, energy code detailing, NFPA 285 for exterior wall assemblies.
5 · Construction Cost Estimates — 2–8% (≈2–8 items) — the smallest section in any of your three divisions
5.1Analyze construction cost estimates to confirm alignment with project designRead an estimate at CD stage and say whether it matches the documents. One objective. Do not overinvest.
