Where the items actually are

Read these as item counts, not percentages. In PPD, section 4 alone is 32–38 items — more than the whole of PA's section 1, 2 and 3 combined.

PA 75 items

Building Analysis & Programming is 28–32 items of 75. It is the single heaviest section in any of your three divisions. Seven objectives live in it. If you are short of time, this is the last thing you cut. Environmental & Contextual Conditions (14–21%) is where you can afford to be merely competent — three objectives, roughly 11–16 items, and most of it is climate-response reasoning you already have from studio.

PPD 100 items

Project Integration of Program & Systems is 32–38 items. It is not a content area you can memorise — it is the section that asks you to hold program, code, structure, MEP and site in your head at once and pick the arrangement that satisfies all of them. Study it by working whole problems, not by reading. Project Costs & Budgeting (8–14%) is the one to be merely competent in, and note it got easier: objective 5.2 was narrowed in April 2026 from creating cost estimates to evaluating them.

PDD 100 items

PDD is a two-headed exam: Construction Documentation (32–38) and Integration of Building Materials & Systems (31–37) are 63–75 of the 100 items between them. Sections 3, 4 and 5 together are only 22–40. Construction Cost Estimates is 2–8 items — two to eight. One objective. Learn what an estimate at CD stage should contain and move on; do not spend a week there.

What changed on 27 April 2026

Twelve of the 91 objectives across all six divisions were revised. Division structure, item counts and testing times did not change, and previously passed divisions are unaffected. Three of the twelve touch your divisions:

ObjectiveChangeWhat it means for you
PA 4.5Narrowed. Now reads "Review and assess the feasibility of the project budget and schedule to meet the project scope."You are no longer asked to create a programming-stage budget or schedule — only to judge whether one that exists is feasible against the scope. Study the sanity-check, not the build-up.
PPD 5.2Narrowed. Now reads "Evaluate cost estimates based on the project design."Shifted from creating estimates to evaluating them. Know what belongs in an estimate at each phase and what accuracy to expect, rather than performing a takeoff.
PPD 5.3Clarified. "Evaluate the project design based on cost considerations."No change in substance. Still design-decision-versus-cost reasoning.

Case studies were also redesigned, effective the same date: each case study now carries 1–2 resources instead of 3–6, with fewer items per case study but roughly the same total case-study items per division. The practical effect is less reading, more reasoning. NCARB reissued the ARE 5.0 Guidelines for April 2026 and released updated practice exams in February 2026. Verified

Correction to material in your folder

Your Amber Book Panic Notes (rev. 2020-05-25) state item counts and testing times for all six divisions — PA "95 questions, 4 hours", PPD and PDD "120 questions, 5 hours". Every one of those figures is superseded. PA is 75 items in 3 hours; PPD and PDD are 100 items in 4 hr 5 min. If you pace yourself off the 2020 numbers you will plan a materially wrong time budget. Item types — three, not six

Two item types have been retired since most of your material was written. Only three remain:

TypeFormScoring
Multiple choiceStem plus 3 or 4 options, select one.1 point, all or nothing.
Check-all-that-applyStem plus 6 options, select all that apply.1 point only if every correct option is selected and no incorrect one is. No partial credit.
HotspotStem plus an image or diagram; click the correct area.1 point if the click lands inside the scoring area.

Quantitative fill-in-the-blank was retired 27 February 2024. Drag-and-place was retired 28 April 2025. Numerical questions still exist — they are now asked as multiple choice, so you calculate and then match your answer to an option. Verified

The CATA arithmetic

Six options, all-or-nothing, no partial credit. If you are 85% confident on each of four selections independently, your chance of scoring the item is about 52%. That is why check-all-that-apply items punish half-knowledge harder than anything else on the exam, and why the flashcard decks here are written as complete lists rather than single facts — a list you know 80% of scores zero.

Scoring is rights-only: one point per item, nothing subtracted for a wrong answer. There is no published penalty for guessing, so never leave an item blank.

Where your folder leaves you exposed

Six gaps, in order of how many items they cost you:

  1. Structural systems — nothing usable. PPD 3.2 and PDD 1.3 both test structural sizing and selection. MEEB has no structural content; Jenny's notes have no structural section; the Ballast structural chapters are unsearchable scans. Filled from outside on the PPD page and the Numbers page, marked Outside.
  2. Egress, occupancy and allowable area at code-text level. Codes & Regulations is 16–22% of PA and 16–22% of PPD. Your only code numbers come from 2020 panic notes and 2012 peer notes, several of which are wrong. Rebuilt from the 2021 IBC and flagged.
  3. Accessibility against the 2010 ADA Standards. AGS discusses ADAAG 1991 and says the 2004 guidelines "will serve as the basis... when adopted." Searching AGS for "2010 ADA Standards" returns zero hits. Rebuilt from ada.gov.
  4. Cost estimating methodology. MEEB covers life-cycle costing only. Problem Seeking has the programming build-up. Nobody in your folder covers estimate classes, escalation, markups or contingency by phase. Filled from AACE and AIA.
  5. Modern HVAC. "Chilled beam" and "DOAS" return zero hits across the entire 8.5 MB of MEEB 11th ed. Both are current practice and testable.
  6. BIM/LOD and the National CAD Standard. No LOD framework anywhere in Olin's, AGS or the PDPG — one passing mention of clash detection. PDD 2.4 asks for documentation standards.

Rules that shape your calendar
  • Retake wait: 60 days. A failed division cannot be retaken for 60 days. If PA goes wrong on 22 October, the earliest retake is 21 December — which collides with your PDD date. Build that risk into the sequence knowingly.
  • Three attempts per division per rolling 12 months.
  • The rolling clock is gone. Retired 30 April 2023; all 55 jurisdictions aligned by 2025. Passed divisions no longer expire after five years — validity is now tied to the life of the exam version.
  • Scores arrive in 7–10 days. A pass report shows no score. A fail report shows a scaled score plus percentage-correct by content area against the average of candidates who passed — that diagnostic is the most useful thing you can get, so read it section by section.
